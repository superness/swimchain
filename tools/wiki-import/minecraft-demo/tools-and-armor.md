# Tools and Armor

Every tool and most armor in [[Minecraft]] belongs to a material tier, and knowing what each tier is *for* saves a lot of wasted resources.

## The tiers

- **Wood** — exists to mine your first stone. Nothing more.
- **Stone** — cheap and everywhere. Perfectly good tools for the entire early game; skip stone armor because it doesn't exist — leather fills that slot.
- **Gold** — mines fast and enchants well but shatters quickly; mostly a novelty in the Overworld, though piglins in [[The Nether]] insist you wear a piece.
- **Iron** — the standard. Full iron tools and armor are the baseline for serious [[Mining]] and [[Combat]].
- **[[Diamond]]** — the reliable top tier: big durability jump, required stepping stone to netherite, and the only pickaxe (besides netherite) that harvests obsidian and ancient debris.
- **[[Netherite]]** — an upgrade applied to diamond gear at a smithing table: stronger, faster, knockback-resistant, and it floats in lava, which is thematically appropriate given where you found it.

## The toolbox

**Pickaxes** break stone and ore — and ore has minimum-tier rules: iron ore needs stone, diamond needs iron, obsidian needs diamond. **Axes** clear wood and double as slow, hard-hitting weapons. **Shovels** move earth and snow. **Hoes** make farmland ([[Farming]]) and harvest leaves and wart quickly. **Shears** collect wool without violence and leaves without loss. **Flint and steel** lights fires and [[The Nether]] portals. The **bucket** — iron's killer app — carries water, lava, and milk, and a water bucket is the best fall-damage insurance in the game.

## Armor and beyond

Armor slots run helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots; each point of the armor bar shaves incoming damage. Leather → iron → diamond → netherite is the practical ladder (chainmail exists mainly as [[Villager]] merchandise). The **shield** blocks nearly everything from the front, including [[Creeper]] blasts. **Elytra** wings from [[The End]] replace your chestplate and change [[Transportation]] forever.

Durability wears with use. Repair at an anvil with matching material, or enchant with Mending ([[Enchanting]]) and let experience orbs do the maintenance.
