# Mobs

**Mobs** — mobile entities — are every creature in [[Minecraft]] that isn't you. They divide roughly into the ones that feed you, the ones that trade with you, and the ones that explode.

## Passive mobs

Cows, sheep, pigs, chickens, and rabbits wander the Overworld as walking supply drops — see [[Farming]] for turning them into infrastructure. Squid ink the oceans, bats squeak in caves, and horses, donkeys, and llamas belong under [[Transportation]]. Fish school in the oceans of the appropriate [[Biomes]], and cats and parrots exist so you can feel guilt about leaving home.

## Neutral mobs

These fight only when provoked, each with its own rulebook. Wolves attack in packs when one is struck — or become loyal dogs if fed bones. Bees sting once, fatally for the bee, so leave the hive alone. Piglins in [[The Nether]] tolerate you only in gold armor; zombified piglins hold grudges collectively; and the [[Enderman]] enforces the strictest rule of all: don't look them in the eye.

## Hostile mobs

Darkness spawns the classic lineup — zombies, skeletons, spiders, witches, and the [[Creeper]], covered in loving detail on its own page. Underwater ruins hold drowned with tridents; deserts add husks; snow adds strays. [[The Nether]] contributes ghasts, blazes, magma cubes, and hoglins. Raids on a [[Villager]] settlement bring pillagers, ravagers, and evokers. Deep underground, the sculk-covered dark hides the warden — a blind horror best answered with sneaking away, not [[Combat]].

## Spawning rules

Hostile mobs spawn in darkness on solid blocks near you; torch light stops them, which is why lighting your base and mines ([[Mining]]) matters more than any weapon. Most burn or crumble under sunrise — creepers, notably, do not. Passive mobs spawn on grass at world generation and persist, which is why breeding beats hunting.

## Bosses

Two mobs get dossiers of their own: the [[Ender Dragon]], the game's finale, and the [[Wither]], the boss you have to build yourself out of bad decisions.
