# Getting Started

Your first day in [[Minecraft]] is a race against the sun. When it sets, hostile [[Mobs]] spawn in the dark — and the difference between a fun first night and a frustrating one is about ten minutes of preparation.

## The first ten minutes

1. **Punch a tree.** Hold the attack button on a trunk until the log breaks. Collect four or five logs.
2. **Make planks and a crafting table.** In your inventory's 2×2 grid, logs become planks and four planks become a [[Crafting]] table — the 3×3 grid almost every recipe needs.
3. **Craft a wooden pickaxe**, then mine the nearest exposed stone. Cobblestone unlocks the full set of stone [[Tools and Armor]] — cheap, fast, and disposable.
4. **Get food.** Break tall grass for wheat seeds, punch a few sheep or cows, or pick sweet berries. Hunger drains as you sprint and jump, and an empty hunger bar stops you from healing.

## Before nightfall

Build a shelter. It genuinely does not need to be good — a hole in a hillside sealed with dirt is a time-honored first home. Craft a **furnace** from eight cobblestone ([[Smelting]]) to cook meat and, if you found iron, smelt it. If you have wool from three sheep plus planks, craft a **bed**: sleeping skips the night entirely and resets your respawn point, which is the single best safety feature available to a new player.

## The first week

- Dig down for coal and iron — see [[Mining]] for how depth works.
- Upgrade to iron gear, then start hunting [[Diamond]].
- Start a small farm ([[Farming]]) so food stops being a daily scramble.
- Trade with a [[Villager]] if a village is nearby; their early deals are modest but their late ones are game-changing.

## What not to do

Don't dig straight down (gravel, lava, and long falls all end the same way), don't fight a [[Creeper]] in a doorway you like, and don't stare at the tall black one — the [[Enderman]] page explains why.
