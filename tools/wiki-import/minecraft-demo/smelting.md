# Smelting

**Smelting** is [[Minecraft]]'s heat-processing system: put an input and a fuel in a furnace, wait, and take out something better. It is how ore becomes ingots, sand becomes glass, and raw chicken stops being a health hazard.

## The furnace family

- **Furnace** (eight cobblestone): smelts everything, the universal default.
- **Blast furnace**: smelts ores and metal items at double speed — but nothing else. The natural upgrade for a [[Mining]] operation.
- **Smoker**: cooks food at double speed and nothing else. One next to your kitchen chest changes early-game life.
- **Campfire**: cooks food slowly without fuel, four items at a time, and doubles as decor.

Blast furnaces and smokers also serve as job-site blocks for the armorer and butcher [[Villager]] professions.

## Fuel

Anything that burns works, but efficiency varies enormously. **Coal and charcoal** are the standard: eight smelts per piece. **Charcoal** — smelted logs — matters because it makes fuel renewable without ever finding coal. A **bucket of lava** is the heavyweight at one hundred smelts. A **block of coal** does eighty. Wooden odds and ends (planks, sticks, saplings, even wooden tools) burn briefly and are best for emergencies. Kelp, once dried and pressed into blocks, powers automated smelter arrays.

## What to smelt

- Raw iron, gold, and copper → ingots ([[Tools and Armor]]).
- Sand → glass; cobblestone → stone; stone → smooth stone; clay → bricks and terracotta.
- Raw meat and potatoes → real food (see [[Farming]] for supply).
- Cactus → green dye; wet sponge → dry sponge; ancient debris → [[Netherite]] scrap.

Smelting also grants experience when you collect the output — a stack of smelted ore is a respectable [[Enchanting]] deposit.

## Automation

Furnaces accept items from **hoppers**: input from the top, fuel from the side, output pulled from the bottom into a chest. A rank of hopper-fed furnaces is many players' first practical [[Redstone]]-adjacent build, and it turns smelting from a chore into a background process.
