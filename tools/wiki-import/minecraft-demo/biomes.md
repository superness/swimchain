# Biomes

**Biomes** are the climates and landscapes that tile the [[Minecraft]] Overworld — each with its own terrain, plants, [[Mobs]], and reasons to visit or flee. World generation blends them along temperature bands, so you'll rarely find snow bordering desert.

## The temperate heartland

Plains, forests, and birch forests are starter country: flat ground, plenty of wood, and most of the [[Villager]] settlements. Dark forests grow roofed canopies dark enough to spawn monsters at noon — and hide woodland mansions. Swamps drip with vines, spawn slimes on full moons, and host witch huts; the mangrove variant adds propagule trees and mud.

## Hot and dry

Deserts are sand, cactus, and husks by night, plus desert temples with loot and a literally explosive floor puzzle. Badlands layer terracotta in painted stripes and riddle their mesas with gold-rich abandoned mineshafts ([[Mining]] loves them). Savannas grow acacia; jungles tower with massive trees, hide temples, and are the only home of cocoa, melons growing wild, parrots, and ocelots.

## Cold and high

Taigas add spruce, sweet berries, foxes, and wolves. Snowy plains and ice spikes trade resources for scenery; powder snow in groves swallows the unwary (leather boots let you climb it). The high peaks generate iron-rich stone and goats with strong opinions about knocking you off cliffs.

## Water worlds

Oceans come in warm, lukewarm, and frozen flavors: coral reefs, kelp forests ([[Smelting]] fuel), shipwrecks, buried treasure, and ocean monuments guarded by beam-firing guardians. Rivers cut everything and are the easiest place to start [[Transportation]] by boat.

## The rare ones

Mushroom fields are the game's oddest prize: no hostile spawns at all, giant mushrooms, and mooshrooms — cows you can bowl for stew. Cherry groves bloom pink; lush caves and deep dark carve their character underground, the latter guarding ancient cities and the warden.

The other dimensions have biomes of their own — see [[The Nether]] and [[The End]].
