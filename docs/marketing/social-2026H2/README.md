## Brand pillars & voice

### No Servers. No Landlord.

Swimchain has no company, no datacenter, no admin panel — every reader runs a node, so the network IS its users. There is nothing to harvest your data into, no feed to sell, no entity to pressure or acquire. If every developer walked away tomorrow, the pool keeps swimming.

**Proof points:**
- Peer-to-peer protocol with zero central infrastructure — every app (Feed, Chat, Forum, Wiki) talks to YOUR node, not a company API
- Mainnet live since July 2026; playable in a plain browser today at swimchain.io/reef, /chess, /browse, /example
- No ads and no data harvesting as an architectural fact, not a policy promise — there is no server to inject ads from or collect data on
- Code and all 13 protocol specs are public on GitHub (superness/swimchain)

### Attention Is Life Support

Nothing here is hoarded forever. Content decays on a 7-day half-life unless people engage with it — the network only carries what communities actively keep alive. Your community's attention, not an archive or an algorithm, decides what persists. Old takes fade; living conversations stay afloat.

**Proof points:**
- 7-day half-life decay is a protocol rule, verifiable in the open specs — not a setting a company can quietly change
- Moderation without moderators: spam and junk rot away on their own when nobody engages; nothing needs a takedown request
- No permanent record weaponized against your past self — casual posts fade, which changes how honestly people talk (the Snapchat lesson, applied to a whole social network)
- Bounded storage is what lets an ordinary phone or PC hold the whole chain — decay is why every user CAN be a node

### Spam Is Expensive by Physics

Every post costs real computational proof-of-work (Argon2id). The pause before publishing is deliberate friction — it breaks the rage-post reflex, makes every contribution cost something, and prices spam out without a single moderator. Swimchain is slow on purpose, the way a good decision is.

**Proof points:**
- Argon2id proof-of-work on every post — a bot farm has to pay the same physics per message as a person does
- No moderation team, no report queue: the economics do the filtering, then decay finishes the job
- Mandatory seconds between impulse and publish — a built-in cooling-off period, the design opposite of the one-tap dopamine loop
- Friction is the anti-engagement-farming stance: platforms remove friction to extract attention; Swimchain adds it to protect yours

### Vouched For, Not Signed Up

Your identity is a cryptographic keypair, and you join because a real member vouches for you — sponsorship, not an email form. No real name required, no data collected, yet every identity has stakes: your sponsor staked their standing on you, and your pseudonym accumulates reputation worth protecting.

**Proof points:**
- Ed25519 keypair identity — no email, no phone number, no legal name anywhere in the protocol
- Sponsorship trees: every new member is vouched for by an existing one, so bot armies can't mass-register — social trust can't be parallelized the way compute can
- Persistent pseudonyms build reputation over time (a la pen names): privacy from real-world targeting, consequences within the community
- How real communities have always grown — someone brings you to the party — made explicit and cryptographic

### Disagree? Fork It.

Swimchain is 13 published specs, not a terms-of-service. There is no CEO to appeal to and no policy team to lobby — if you think the rules should be different, fork the protocol and take your community with you. Even chain selection is a spec. Exit is a right, built in.

**Proof points:**
- 13 written protocol specifications, all public on GitHub (superness/swimchain) — the rules are legible, auditable, and forkable
- Fork-friendly by design: a captured or 51%-attacked chain gets abandoned, not endured — the community leaves, the attacker wins an empty pool
- No algorithm anywhere in the protocol: clients can render feeds, forums, or chat, but no hidden system can decide what you see
- Governance by evolution, not politics: better rule-sets attract communities; bad ones lose them

## Truth guardrails

Flagged by the review pass — standing rules for every future post:

- '~30 seconds of proof-of-work' — appears in ALL of campaigns 1, 2, 3, and 5 (e.g. C1 FB wk4 'takes your computer about 30 seconds of real work', C2 LI wk5 'roughly 30 seconds of real, memory-hard computation', C3 FB wk5, C5 FB wk2/LI wk7). The fact sheet specifies Argon2id proof-of-work with NO duration. This is the single most-repeated invented number in the portfolio; strip it everywhere until a measured figure is signed off.
- 'It runs on 1 CPU and 1 GB of RAM' — Campaign 4, LI wk6 launcher post. Invented hardware spec, not on the fact sheet.
- 'Play the bot' — Campaigns 3 (FB wk3) and 4 (FB wk3) chess posts. The fact sheet says chess games live on-chain; it says nothing about a bot opponent. Verify or cut.
- 'There's no algorithm at all' (C3 FB wk7) and 'no algorithm anywhere in the protocol' (C1 LI wk8, C4 LI wk1) — overreach. Decay, reputation, and spam attestation are algorithms; the true claim is 'no ranking algorithm / no engagement auction.'
- 'A 51%-attacked chain doesn't have to be endured — it gets abandoned' — Campaign 2, LI wk8 (and Dec beat). Security doctrine not in the fact sheet, stated as settled fact.
- 'There is no password reset... lose your keys and that identity is gone' / 'no key recovery' — Campaign 2, LI wk2 and Nov beat. The protocol has a recovery seed; correct claim is 'no company reset — your seed is the only recovery.'
- Campaign 5 LI wk7 narrates tester findings ('sync on hotel wifi', 'what a 30-second proof-of-work feels like on mid-range hardware') as if closed testing has already produced them — anticipatory experience presented as field notes.
- 'Spam-flagged content collapses to a 4-hour half-life' (C1, C2) — NOT on the fact sheet, but verified true in the project docs (7-day half-life, 4 hours if spam-flagged). No copy change needed; add it to the fact sheet so future writers aren't guessing.
- 'Archiver client' (C1 LI wk1) and 'archiver, bridge, analytics client' (C4 LI wk8) — real in the public repo but absent from the fact sheet's app list; keep only with the build-from-source qualifier so they never read as shipped downloads.

Session verification notes: the chess bot opponent is REAL (chess-bot-mainnet service); "1 CPU and 1 GB of RAM is plenty" is on the live landing page — both claims usable. Never: token/web3 wording, dates, user counts, Play Store availability (only "working toward it").
