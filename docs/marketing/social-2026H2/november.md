## Week 3 — No Server Behind This Chat Room

**LinkedIn**

There is no server behind this chat room.

Swimchain's Chat client looks like Discord — channels, rooms, real-time messages. But underneath, there's no company, no server farm, no terms-of-service pivot waiting to happen. The room is carried by the nodes of the people in it.

Which changes some things you may not have thought about:

Nobody can acquire your community, because there's nothing to buy.

Nobody can shut it down, because there's nothing to shut down.

Nobody reads your messages to price ads against them, because there are no ads and no reader.

The tradeoff — and we state tradeoffs proudly — is that sending a message costs your machine a moment of real computational work. Seconds, usually. That small cost is the entire anti-spam system, and it keeps the noise floor low without a moderation department.

## Week 4 — If Words Had to Earn Their Keep

**LinkedIn**

If every post cost real work and started fading within a week unless someone cared — what would you write?

That's not a hypothetical; it's the actual physics of Swimchain. Posting requires proof-of-work — real computation, seconds to half a minute. And content decays with a 7-day half-life unless people engage with it.

Every client obeys the same physics: the forum thread, the chat message, the wiki page, the feed item. Same chain, same decay, everywhere. What communities care about survives; what they don't fades away.

That changes the calculus of posting. When there's a small real cost and no permanent archive by default, throwaway takes get thrown away before they're written. What's left is what someone actually wanted to say.

So, honest question — no agenda beyond curiosity:

If your words cost effort and only survived by mattering to someone, what would you still write? And what would you stop writing?
