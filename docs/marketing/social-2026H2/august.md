## Week 2 — Posting Costs Something. On Purpose.

**LinkedIn**

On Swimchain, posting costs something. On purpose.

Every post requires proof-of-work: your computer does a few seconds of real computation — sometimes up to thirty — before your words go anywhere.

That's friction. Deliberately.

When posting is free, spam is free. Bot farms are free. A million-message ad campaign is free. The economics of every major platform quietly favor whoever can post the most, and it isn't you.

Flip the cost model and all of that inverts. A human sharing a thought a few times a day barely notices the work. A spammer posting ten thousand times a day is setting money on fire. Spam doesn't get moderated away — it becomes economically pointless.

This is a real tradeoff, and we'll state it plainly: Swimchain is slower to post to than Instagram is to scroll. We think a network where every message cost someone something reads very differently from one where none did.

Would you trade thirty seconds for a network where spam doesn't pay?

## Week 3 — Everything Here Is Disappearing

**LinkedIn**

Everything posted on Swimchain is disappearing right now. Slowly. By design.

Content on the network has a seven-day half-life. Left alone, a post fades away. But when people engage with it — read it, reply, build on it — it stays alive. What a community cares about survives. What nobody cares about quietly composts.

Compare that to every platform you've used, where a decade of hot takes, dead memes, and abandoned arguments sits in a warehouse forever, waiting to be mined, resurfaced, or used against someone.

Decay changes how a network feels. There's no infinite archive to doomscroll. Attention is life support — communities keep alive exactly the things they keep touching. A garden, not a landfill.

It also changes what you write. When permanence isn't the default, the goal isn't to go viral once. It's to matter to the people who'll keep the thing alive.

What would you write differently if "forever" wasn't the default?
