## Week 1 — Attention Is Life Support

**LinkedIn**

On most platforms, everything you ever posted is still there — being mined, resold, and repackaged.

On Swimchain, content decays. Seven-day half-life. If nobody engages, it fades out of the network. Not archived. Gone.

That sounds like a bug until you sit with it. Garbage doesn't accumulate. Old arguments don't follow you around forever. Nothing piles up to be harvested, because the network keeps only what communities actively keep alive.

We made this visible in Reef, a browser game where every coral tile is a post. Tend it and it thrives. Walk away and it fades. Attention is life support — the protocol just says it out loud.

There's no company behind this, no servers, no ads, no algorithm deciding what survives. Just people, running nodes, deciding what's worth keeping.

What would you post if you knew it would fade unless it mattered to someone?

## Week 2 — Chess With No Server

**LinkedIn**

There's a chess game at swimchain.io where the board has no server.

Every move lives on Swimchain, a social protocol run entirely by the people using it. No company hosts the match. No central database stores it. The game sits on the chain, held up by the same network of user-run nodes that holds up everything else.

Why chess? Because it's the cleanest possible demo of a strange claim: two people don't need a company in the middle to share a space that neither of them — and no one else — can quietly rewrite. If a protocol can hold a chess match together, it can hold a forum, a wiki, a newspaper.

And like everything on Swimchain, finished games are subject to decay — a match nobody revisits eventually fades, the way old games probably should.

Open source. Pseudonymous cryptographic identities. Thirteen published protocol specs — every rule a node enforces is written down.

Play a match in your browser: swimchain.io.

## Week 2 — What Would You Stop Posting?

**LinkedIn**

If posting cost your computer thirty seconds of real work, what would you stop posting?

On Swimchain, every post requires proof-of-work — a few seconds to about thirty of actual computation. It's not a paywall; there's nothing to buy and no one to pay. It's friction, on purpose.

Friction kills spam, because spam only works at zero cost. It kills bot swarms and ad campaigns for the same reason. What's left is people who meant it.

We won't pretend this is effortless. It's harder than scrolling, and that's the point — we'd rather reach a thousand people who get it than a hundred thousand who churn.

So, honestly: what would you stop posting? And what might finally feel worth saying?

## Week 3 — Your Node Is Your Lantern

**LinkedIn**

In The Trench, your lantern only burns while your node is running.

It's a homestead game for Windows: you settle a patch of the deep sea, and the light you tend is literal infrastructure — the Swimchain node on your machine, helping keep a serverless social network alive. Switch your node off and your corner of the deep goes dark.

We built it because "run your own node" is the least appealing sentence in software. Nobody wakes up wanting to run infrastructure. But people do wake up wanting to tend something — a garden, a fire, a homestead.

Swimchain has no company and no servers. The network is its users, which means somebody has to actually be the network. The Trench makes that job feel like what it is: keeping a light on for other people in the dark.

Fair warning: this is more involved than installing another feed to scroll. That's rather the point.

It ships in the free Windows desktop app alongside a forum, chat, a feed built by your own node, and a collaborative wiki. Get it at swimchain.io/download.
