When Swimchain reaches Google Play, nothing about the network will change. That sentence is stranger than it looks.

For a conventional app, the store is one link in a chain of dependencies: store, app, API, company, servers. Remove any link and the product dies. For Swimchain, the store is just a doorway. Behind it is a protocol — 13 public specifications, code on GitHub, a network that consists entirely of its users' nodes. The Android app is one doorway among several: a browser tab today, a Windows desktop launcher, a signed APK you can sideload, and eventually a Play listing.

This is why we can narrate the Play process without anxiety about its timeline. If review takes long, the network keeps swimming. If a store policy shifted tomorrow, the APK would still be on our site — and the protocol would still be forkable by anyone who disagrees with us. Chain selection is a spec too.

We pursue the store anyway, because doorways matter: most people will only ever enter through the front one. The next months of closed testing are about making that entrance worthy of what's behind it.
